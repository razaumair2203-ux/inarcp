TITLE
Innovation-Normalized Detection in Compound-Gaussian Clutter: Exact Laws, Conformal Thresholds and Certified Integration under Pulsed Interference

ABSTRACT
Classical cell-averaging and order-statistic constant-false-alarm-rate (CFAR) laws assume independent exponential samples. Correlated, textured clutter can violate this premise. Autoregressive (AR) whitening and innovation-power normalization recover these laws under the true AR compound-Gaussian model with a common episode texture and no thermal noise. We derive a post-onset law for persistent target self-masking; a slow-time guard delays the loss by its length. Injected-target trajectories show delayed collapse through 48 looks. Under exchangeability and a dominating, clutter-independent hit model, a conformal threshold on a worst-case bound certifies clipped and binary integration against pulsed interference of any power. On McMaster IPIX radar sea-clutter data, conformal thresholds keep 12 of 13 statistics within 0.73–1.47 times design down to 10^(-4); 10 of 11 model-based laws exceed twice design, up to 60-fold. At 10^(-2), an eight-pulse guard raises mean per-look detection of an injected persistent 10 dB target over looks 1–8 from 0.24 to 0.82, except at clutter Doppler. With 5% independent pulse hits, certified integration stays below design with detection probability 0.64; uncertified integration exceeds design up to 23-fold. On NetRAD S-band sea clutter, unused in development, the guard raises mean detection from 0.30 to 0.92; certified integration gives 0.08 times design and detection 0.92 under the same injected-hit model. With refitted conformal thresholds, all 13 statistics stay within 1.36 times design at 10^(-3); five exceed twice design at 10^(-4). Burst-matched certification controls false alarms but detects no target. Guarded IN-ARCP has low acquisition probability under idealized continuous migration.
