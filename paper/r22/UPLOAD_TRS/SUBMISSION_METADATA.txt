TITLE
Innovation-Normalized Detection in Compound-Gaussian Clutter: Exact Laws, Conformal Thresholds and Certified Integration under Pulsed Interference

ABSTRACT
Classical cell-averaging and order-statistic constant-false-alarm-rate (CFAR) laws assume independent exponential samples. Correlated, textured clutter can violate this premise. Autoregressive (AR) whitening and innovation-power normalization recover these laws under the true AR compound-Gaussian model with common episode texture and no thermal noise. We derive a post-onset law quantifying persistent-target contamination of a cell's own history; a slow-time guard delays self-masking by its length. Under exchangeability and a dominating, clutter-independent hit model, a conformal threshold on a worst-case bound certifies clipped and binary integration against pulsed interference of any power. Simulations and measurements of sea clutter and 77 GHz ground scenes test calibration, target visibility, noise and interference. On McMaster IPIX sea clutter, conformal thresholds keep 12 of 13 statistics within 0.73–1.47 times design down to 10^(-4); 10 of 11 model-based laws exceed twice design, up to 60-fold. At 10^(-2), an eight-pulse guard raises mean per-look detection of an injected persistent 10 dB target over looks 1–8 from 0.24 to 0.82, except at clutter Doppler; 48-look trajectories show later collapse. The guard benefit recurs on NetRAD S-band clutter, unused in initial development; five statistics exceed twice design at 10^(-4) after threshold refitting. With sparse real 77 GHz interference at 10^(-2), certified clipping gives 0.54 times design and costs 6.4 dB versus clean-threshold clipping; fast-time zeroing restores clean-reference rates. Radar- and camera-labelled pedestrians show little whitening advantage at weak inter-frame correlation. Guarded acquisition remains poor under idealized continuous migration; burst-matched certification controls false alarms but detects no target.
