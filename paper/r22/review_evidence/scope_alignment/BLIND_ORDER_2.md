## Evidence for the preference

1. **The abstract now describes the actual complementary measurement roles.** The first abstract concentrates on IPIX/NetRAD calibration, injected targets and independent injected interference. Those statements are supported, but it leaves out the ground measurements that occupy substantive portions of Sections V and VI. The second adds that sea clutter and 77 GHz ground scenes test calibration, target visibility, noise and interference, then reports sparse *real* 77 GHz interference at `0.54` times design with a `6.4 dB` sensitivity cost and notes little pedestrian whitening advantage at weak inter-frame correlation. These are directly supported by Section VI-D (p. 8) and VI-B (p. 7). The practical reader gains the distinction between correlated-clutter sensitivity, real-interference behavior, and weak-correlation target checks. The second does not claim that the pedestrian checks validate useful onset detection: the body retains the seven-block JKU limitation and the UW lack of eligible primary clean entries.

2. **The mathematical hook is more concrete without changing the mature mathematics.** First: “a post-onset law for persistent target self-masking.” Second: a law “quantifying persistent-target contamination of a cell's own history.” This connects the contribution to the defined denominator and Fig. 1, where the scale window is guarded but the predictor retains the latest sample. The unchanged Proposition 3 establishes eventual strong-target self-masking; Proposition 4 and Corollary 4 quantify the partially contaminated history and the finite horizon; the guard shifts that horizon under the stated model. The second's conclusion calls the 48-look trajectories confirmation of the “finite benefit of guards,” matching Fig. 3b and VI-C rather than implying indefinite target visibility. The exact-law assumptions remain true AR whitening, common episode texture and no thermal noise. No change to this mathematical body is needed.

3. **The discussion and conclusion give ground tests their supported purpose while preserving epistemic limits.** The first discussion says Table IV separates calibration, sensitivity and failure mechanisms. The second connects detector choice to correlation, noise, history contamination and pulse clustering, explicitly calling sea and ground tests complementary. This interpretation follows the body: VI-A demonstrates noise-dependent calibration, VI-B weak frame correlation, VI-C history contamination, and VI-D burst/hit-model limitations. The revised conclusion also explicitly retains common texture, names JKU noise and real-interference results, and explains limited gain in JKU/UW pedestrian checks. These are a clearer synthesis of the existing work, not evidence of transfer of sea-clutter tail calibration to ground scenes.

4. **The certificate remains conditional and the negative operational boundaries remain prominent.** Both abstracts retain exchangeability and a dominating, clutter-independent hit model. The unchanged Theorem 1 specifies inclusion-order mask dominance and arbitrary interference amplitudes; the adjacent text explains why equal hit rate does not establish dominance. The second discussion explicitly says measured agreement and empirical masks do not prove those assumptions. It retains approximate within-session support, online target contamination, injected timing/alignment, descriptive real-target evidence, remaining NetRAD failures at `10^-4`, useless burst-matched detection and poor continuous-migration acquisition. The final sentence still requires independently timed real-target onsets in correlated clutter and a measured range response for operational validation. The second's confident empirical wording is therefore appropriately bounded.

5. **The table changes improve identification and traceability within the same page budget.** First Table IV labels a row “Real ground targets”; second specifies “UW camera-associated pedestrians,” consistent with VI-B's presence-association measure rather than precise native-bin onset labels. “Real interference, 77 GHz” becomes “JKU real interference, 77 GHz.” The second caption adds supplementary evidence locations while retaining rate definitions, injected SCR, guard comparisons, cluster intervals and unequal realized false-alarm rates. Visual inspection found no clipping, overlap or material readability loss from these changes. Both abstracts remain dense but legible; the second replaces some repeated injected-hit numbers with otherwise missing scope information. The figure captions and central body are already mature.
